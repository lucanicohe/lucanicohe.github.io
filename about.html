Origins

Leaving my town, for Japan.

I grew up in a small town in Germany, where young people have few opportunities and many leave. I wanted to build a future rather than watch the town fade, and that pushed me to look further.

Growing up on anime, I fell in love with Japanese culture and history. Living in Japan and one day contributing something back has been a dream for a long time. Game development is where my technical skills and creativity meet, so that is the path I’m building.

In 2023 I made the move to Japan.

Craft

Technique, and how it looks.

I’m not only about the technical side. My brother is an artist, and since I was a child he has taught me 3D software, photography and editing. Because of him I care not just about making things work, but about making them look beautiful.

I work across systems, design and visuals, and I like owning every part of what I make.

I see myself as an artist first. I'm studying game programming at TECH.C., and I don't mind working as a game programmer. But technical art is what excites me most: using Unity, Unreal Engine and code as tools to make games look beautiful through shaders, lighting, effects and environments. I'm also very interested in design roles, where I can shape the look and feel of a game.

Language school · 2023 – 2025

Learning Japanese.

After moving to Japan in 2023, I enrolled at Kai Language School and studied Japanese for two years, finishing at Level N2 in 2025.

It opened the door to the next step: entering a Japanese technical college.

Now · TECH.C.

Make it. Finish it.

Since 2025 I’ve been studying game development at TECH.C., with graduation planned for 2028.

Ideas are easy. Building them, solving the problems and finishing them is the hard part. I make games to practice exactly that: taking an idea all the way to something people can actually play, and making it fun and memorable for others, not just for me.

Every project teaches me at least one new thing.

Hobbies

My hobbies.

Games

I have played games since I was young, starting on the Nintendo DS. Later, my dad gave me his old work computer, and I started exploring PC games, beginning with Western games. Over time, I found my way to Japanese games.

D.LEAGUE

An artist friend I know from Instagram, who works with a company involved in D.LEAGUE, invited me and showed it to me. I found it fascinating, especially because of its connection to game companies like SEGA (SEGA SAMMY LUX) and KADOKAWA (KADOKAWA DREAMS).

Perspective

Well made is now easy. Character is not.

AI can write code, draw, model and texture, and it does it all well. Making something look good is no longer the hard part. Giving it a character of its own is.

That's why I see myself as an artist. Someday AI will do everything, except be someone. So I put myself into everything I make, something no one else can copy, because there is only one of me.

Next

A Japanese company first. Then my own.

I'd like to join a company first and learn how professional teams work. One day I want to start my own, make things many people love, and give something back to Japan.

I'm open to part-time work now and full-time work from 2028.
